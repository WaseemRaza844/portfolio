Dear Members of the Search Committee:

I am writing to apply for the Adjunct Faculty position in Computer Science at William Paterson University. I earned my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering from the University of Oklahoma in 2025, specializing in artificial intelligence and machine learning for reliable wireless networks. My background combines undergraduate teaching, hands-on laboratory instruction, research mentoring, and industry work at AT&T, AT&T Labs, Futurewei, and Impac Exploration Services. I am especially interested in contributing to **Computer and Information Technology** and **Fundamentals of Computer Networking**, where I can connect academic foundations with contemporary computing and telecommunications practice.

I previously served as a Lecturer in the Department of Technology at the University of Lahore, teaching **Computer Programming, Communication Systems, and Wireless Communication** to undergraduate engineering technology students. I prepared instructional materials and assessments, advised student projects, and helped students connect theoretical concepts with implementation. At the University of Engineering and Technology, Taxila, I supported communications instruction and laboratory exercises, while my research appointment at COMSATS University Islamabad involved mentoring Bachelor's and Master's students on technical projects, simulations, and research communication. These experiences shaped an instructional approach centered on clear learning objectives, progressive hands-on exercises, and constructive feedback.

My academic preparation is particularly relevant to networking education. At the University of Oklahoma, I investigated cellular network modeling, wireless propagation, optimization, outage management, and resilient communications, developing simulation and machine-learning workflows to study network behavior under realistic constraints. This research offers practical examples for teaching network architectures, communication protocols, network performance, troubleshooting, and the relationship between wired and wireless systems. I would structure course activities around interpretable examples, reproducible network experiments, and increasingly independent technical assignments.

In industry, my current machine-learning engineering work at AT&T supports telecommunications service assurance and network diagnostics. I have developed data-driven root-cause classification workflows that improved automated classification performance from approximately 20% to 80%, while working with operational network data and collaborating across technical teams. Earlier work at AT&T Labs and Futurewei provided exposure to wireless research and 5G systems; at Impac Exploration Services, I developed cloud-based data engineering and analysis workflows. Together, these roles allow me to introduce students to real engineering practices involving Python, data systems, software development, validation, and responsible interpretation of results.

Beyond my two primary course interests, I would be willing to teach **Discrete Structures** after reviewing the approved course outline and preparing course-specific instructional materials. My mathematical and computational background includes algorithms, modeling, probability, and analytical problem solving, although I do not present Discrete Structures as a previously taught course. I would welcome either in-person or online teaching assignments, subject to scheduling and departmental needs, and would provide accessible student support, timely feedback, and clearly communicated expectations.

William Paterson University's commitment to applied education, professional preparation, and student success aligns with my aim to make technical computing concepts both rigorous and approachable. I would be pleased to contribute to the department as opportunities become available. Thank you for considering my application; I welcome the opportunity to discuss my teaching interests and relevant academic and professional experience.
