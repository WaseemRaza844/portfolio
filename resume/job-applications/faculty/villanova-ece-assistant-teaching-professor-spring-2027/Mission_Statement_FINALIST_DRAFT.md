# Statement of Contribution to Villanova University's Mission

> Waseem Raza, PhD | Finalist-stage draft | Assistant Teaching Professor in Electrical Engineering

Villanova University's Augustinian mission identifies **Veritas, Unitas, and Caritas—Truth, Unity, and Love** as enduring commitments to learning and service. I understand these not simply as institutional ideals, but as habits that can shape the daily work of an engineering educator: pursuing sound evidence, learning with others, and using technical knowledge responsibly. I would seek to contribute to those commitments through undergraduate and graduate teaching, student mentoring, and service to the College of Engineering.

**Truth** begins in engineering with intellectual honesty. Students need to distinguish a model from the real system, check assumptions, report uncertainty, and acknowledge when experiments do not support a prediction. In my teaching, I would ask students to derive or explain a result, test it in simulation or a laboratory, and discuss limitations openly. My research in wireless network reliability and sensor-based learning has reinforced the importance of understanding failure cases rather than presenting only average success. I want students to carry that discipline into their professional lives.

**Unity** means helping students learn both independently and as members of a supportive academic community. As a Lecturer in the University of Lahore's Department of Technology, and through laboratory support and student mentoring at UET Taxila and COMSATS University Islamabad, I worked with students whose preparation and confidence varied. I learned the importance of approachable explanations, clear expectations, constructive feedback, and giving different students meaningful roles in project work. At Villanova, I would contribute to team-based design, accessible office hours, and coordinated advising that connects coursework, capstone experiences, and career preparation.

**Love**, understood as care for others and attention to the common good, asks an engineer to consider whom a design serves and who may be harmed when it fails. My applied work in telecommunications and critical service assurance has made reliability and responsibility particularly tangible. I would encourage students to ask how engineering decisions affect users, communities, sustainability, accessibility, and safety. I would welcome opportunities to participate in student service activities or community-engaged engineering projects that align with departmental needs and available partnerships.

I respect Villanova's Catholic and Augustinian identity and its welcoming community of people with varied beliefs and experiences. My contribution would be to support rigorous inquiry, mutual respect, thoughtful dialogue, and service through the everyday work of teaching, advising, and collaboration. I hope students would leave my classroom not only better prepared technically, but also more capable of using their expertise with humility, integrity, and care.
