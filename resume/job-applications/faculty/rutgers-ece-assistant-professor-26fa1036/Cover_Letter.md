**Re: Assistant Professor Tenure Track AY – Electrical and Computer Engineering (26FA1036)**

Dear Members of the Search Committee:

I am applying for the tenure-track Assistant Professor position in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Rutgers University–New Brunswick (posting 26FA1036). I earned my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Oklahoma in July 2025, where I researched machine learning for reliable and resilient wireless networks. My scholarship, teaching, and applied industry experience position me at the intersection of two areas identified in your search: **AI for next-generation networking** and **healthcare technology through intelligent sensing**. I would welcome the opportunity to develop an externally supported, student-centered research program in those areas while contributing to Rutgers' undergraduate and graduate education.

My doctoral research addressed the reliability of data-driven network decisions when propagation measurements are scarce, radio environments change, or service disruptions occur. I investigated generative and transfer learning for wireless propagation digital twins, 3GPP-aligned cellular simulation, and learning-based outage management and optimization. This work contributed to peer-reviewed journal and conference publications, including IEEE PIMRC and research on proactive cellular-network reliability. At AT&T Labs, I worked on 5G standalone timing analysis, NR-NTN cell-selection algorithms, and large-scale network diagnostics; at Futurewei, I investigated transfer learning for channel-state-information compression. My current machine learning engineering assignment supporting AT&T's E911 service-assurance workflows connects these interests to operational data quality, model validation, and public-safety reliability.

A complementary line of research concerns trustworthy AI for wearable sensing. My smartwatch-based work on medication-taking recognition and dietary-intake detection investigates motion signals, generalization across people, sequential decisions, and false alarms. At Rutgers, I envision a cohesive program on **reliable intelligence under changing conditions**, with one direction developing adaptive models for wireless infrastructure and a second studying uncertainty-aware AI for healthcare sensors. A unifying methodological theme is data-efficient learning, rigorous evaluation, and safe operation when a model encounters a new context. I would pursue collaborative, interdisciplinary projects and appropriate external funding opportunities, while building a publication pipeline with undergraduate and graduate researchers.

My teaching experience includes undergraduate Computer Programming, Communication Systems, and Wireless Communication as a Lecturer at the University of Lahore, communications laboratory support at UET Taxila, and research mentoring at COMSATS and the University of Oklahoma. I could contribute to communications, computer networking, signal processing, applied AI/ML, and intelligent sensing courses, including project-oriented laboratories. My approach emphasizes connecting theory to reproducible experiments, learning from failure modes, and helping students communicate engineering tradeoffs.

Rutgers' emphasis on physical and virtual infrastructure, healthcare technology, and interdisciplinary scholarship provides a compelling environment for this research and teaching agenda. I would be enthusiastic about contributing to departmental and university service, advising, curriculum development, and collaborative initiatives. Thank you for considering my application.

Sincerely,

Waseem Raza, PhD
