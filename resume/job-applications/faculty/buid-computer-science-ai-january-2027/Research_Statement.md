# Research Statement — Trustworthy, Data-Efficient and Applied Artificial Intelligence

Waseem Raza, PhD | The British University in Dubai | Proposed Research Programme, 2027–2030

## Research foundations and contributions

My research develops AI/ML methods that remain effective when **training data are scarce, operating conditions change, or predictions have real consequences**. My PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Oklahoma (2025) focused on AI/ML for wireless networks, with contributions in robust propagation modelling, digital twins, generative augmentation, transfer/domain adaptation, network anomaly detection, and learning-based network recovery. I have published in peer-reviewed venues including *Computer Communications*, *Sensors*, *IEEE Network*, IEEE PIMRC and IEEE VTC. Applied work at AT&T, AT&T Labs and Futurewei has complemented this scholarship with constraints encountered in deployed systems: noisy operational data, changing domains, explainability, testing, and reliability.

Recent work also extends these questions beyond telecommunications. Research in smartwatch-based medication adherence and dietary-intake recognition applies deep learning and sequence modelling to human-generated sensor streams, where inter-user variability and background activities create significant distribution shifts. Across these applications, a consistent methodological theme is rigorous evaluation: held-out environments or users, meaningful baselines, uncertainty, failure-mode analysis, and the relationship between predictive performance and system-level utility.

## Research direction 1 — Robust and trustworthy AI

I will investigate reliable learning under distribution shift, sparse labels, and imperfect sensing. The programme will compare domain adaptation, self-supervised and transfer learning, uncertainty quantification, calibration, and selective prediction. Instead of reporting only average accuracy, research will measure performance degradation under controlled shifts, confidence reliability, error asymmetries, and compute costs. Early student projects will build benchmark pipelines and audit existing models; more advanced work will investigate new adaptation or selective-inference methods.

## Research direction 2 — Generative and data-efficient learning

A second direction explores when generative models and synthetic data can improve performance without introducing unrealistic patterns or privacy risks. Building on my doctoral work with domain-informed GANs for propagation modelling, I will examine validation of synthetic samples, data-efficient training, and model robustness. A parallel applied track can explore retrieval-augmented generation and agentic AI for engineering knowledge and operations, emphasizing grounding, reproducible evaluation, human oversight, and transparent failure analysis. These projects connect current AI methods with measurable research questions rather than assuming that model size alone produces innovation.

## Research direction 3 — AI for intelligent sensing and autonomous networked systems

The third direction creates application testbeds for trustworthy learning. In wireless systems, experiments will study failure detection, resource optimisation, and reinforcement learning for resilient operations. In wearable sensing, time-series classification and sequence modelling will address person-to-person generalisation, false positives, and privacy-preserving inference. These projects offer opportunities to collaborate with colleagues in computer science, cybersecurity, applied engineering, and health-oriented computing. They can also lead to student capstones or postgraduate dissertations using public or appropriately authorised datasets; any new participant study would follow institutional ethics approval.

## Funding, partnerships and postgraduate supervision

In the first 12 months, I would establish two reproducible benchmark streams, recruit undergraduate and MSc students through scoped projects, and prepare one or more collaborative proposals. In years two and three, I would develop external partnerships and pursue competitive opportunities relevant to UAE priorities in responsible AI, digital transformation, and intelligent infrastructure, as well as internal seed funding where available. Specific calls, budgets, and eligibility would be confirmed with BUiD's research office before submission; I do not claim prior awards as principal investigator.

I would structure supervision around weekly meetings, milestone-based plans, code and data documentation, responsible research practice, and appropriate publication authorship. Undergraduate students could contribute to validated baselines and demonstrators; MSc students could investigate focused methodological questions; and PhD students could develop original approaches through co-supervision with faculty colleagues. I would target peer-reviewed journal articles and strong international conferences while avoiding fragmented or unreproducible outputs.

The overall goal is a research programme recognised for technically rigorous, societally relevant AI—one that advances machine-learning methods while producing meaningful teaching, student training, and industry collaboration outcomes at BUiD.
