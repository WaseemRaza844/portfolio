Dear Members of the Search Committee:

I am applying for the tenure-track Assistant Professor position in Data Science in Seton Hall University's Department of Mathematics and Computer Science, beginning August 2027. I earned my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Oklahoma in 2025, where I studied AI and machine learning for reliable wireless networks. My preparation combines peer-reviewed research on data-efficient and robust learning, undergraduate university teaching and student mentorship, and applied data-science engineering in industry. I am particularly drawn to a position that integrates computer science teaching, undergraduate and graduate data-science education, active scholarship, and student research.

My research examines how machine-learning systems generalize when training data are limited, noisy, or drawn from environments different from those in which a model is deployed. I have developed and evaluated transfer-learning, generative-learning, anomaly-detection, and optimization methods in wireless networks and related data-intensive settings. This work has resulted in peer-reviewed publications in communications and intelligent-systems venues. I have also worked on time-series recognition using smartwatch sensors, where differences among participants and daily activities create meaningful challenges for reliable inference. At Seton Hall, I would develop these contributions into a focused data-science research agenda in robust model evaluation, data-efficient learning, and trustworthy time-series analytics, with research questions appropriate for student collaboration and external funding applications.

I previously taught undergraduate Computer Programming, Communication Systems, and Wireless Communication at the University of Lahore, supported laboratory teaching at UET Taxila, and mentored student research at COMSATS and the University of Oklahoma. I would contribute most immediately to introductory programming, Python and data analysis, machine learning, applied statistics for computing, and data-science projects. I am also willing to prepare foundational computer science courses according to departmental needs, without assuming that my earlier teaching covered every part of the CS curriculum. My approach connects conceptual explanation, small reproducible experiments, useful feedback, and project-based assessment.

In my current ML engineering assignment supporting AT&T's E911 service-assurance operations, I have contributed to a root-cause classification workflow whose automated performance improved from approximately 20% to 80%. Earlier work at Impac Exploration Services and AT&T Labs provided experience with Azure data pipelines, large diagnostic datasets, model validation, and communicating analytical evidence to multidisciplinary teams. These experiences would inform realistic student projects using open or synthetic datasets rather than proprietary information.

I welcome Seton Hall's emphasis on academic excellence, inclusion, ethical development, and service. I would work to make research opportunities accessible to students with varied preparation and mentor students through research questions, code review, responsible data practice, writing, and presentation. Enclosed are my CV and statements of teaching, research, and diversity; I have also prepared a separate, unsigned reference-letter access waiver for the application process. Thank you for your consideration.
